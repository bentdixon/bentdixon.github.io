I'm Benjamin, an MSc student in Cognitive Science at Edinburgh University (School of Informatics) and Research Scientist at the Language as a Biomarker Lab under Dr. Wolff.

My current research focuses on developing computational analyses of speech and pairing those analyses with neural data to discover novel biomarkers of neuropsychiatric and neurodegenerative disorders. My research also involves machine learning and representation alignment. My interests include computational psychiatry, neural time series data, and information-theoretic approaches to language.

Outside of academia, I want to help build technologies that are useful for disabled people.

Projects

Word Surprisal and EEG Encoding Models

Independent project

2026
  • Tested whether language-model surprisal predicts EEG from 19 people listening to an audiobook, using ridge encoding models (temporal response functions).
  • Surprisal improved prediction in 18 of 19 participants (p < 0.001) beyond acoustic, timing and frequency controls, localized to a centro-parietal response at about 400 ms (N400).

Code

tpg-onan: A Language Model That Reads Backwards

Independent project

2026
  • Pretrained two identical nanochat base models on the same data, one predicting the next token and one predicting the previous token, so text can be generated in both directions.
  • Predicting the past was consistently but only slightly harder: about 2.5–3% higher validation bits per byte at 537M and 897M parameters.
